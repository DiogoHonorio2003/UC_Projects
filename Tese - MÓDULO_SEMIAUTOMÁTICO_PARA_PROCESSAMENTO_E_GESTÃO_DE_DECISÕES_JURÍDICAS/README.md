This dissertation presents the design, development, integration, and validation of a new module dedicated to the semi-automatic management of legal decisions in Datajuris' BackOffice, developed within the context of the AI4Juris project. This work was carried out as part of a curricular internship at Instituto Pedro Nunes and aims to address the absence of a specific module for managing the decisions included in the law reports, as well as the strong dependence on manual processes for document insertion and the identification of their respective data.

The developed solution introduces a dedicated law report module that supports the management of legal decisions and their associated entities. In addition to the most common management operations, the module enables the import of both individual DOCX documents and sets of documents through ZIP files. Relevant metadata and legal entities from the imported documents are automatically extracted and used to pre-fill the corresponding fields. The extracted information is presented alongside the original document, allowing the user to review, correct, complete, and validate the results before their final persistence.

This approach therefore establishes a semi-automatic workflow, providing a more automated extraction process to assist the user while maintaining human supervision over the final data. The solution was integrated with the existing BackOffice, API, and database architecture and was evaluated in a local environment in terms of performance, scalability, security, success rate in extration, efficiency, and usability.

The processing of 100 documents through a ZIP file achieved an average processing time of approximately two minutes. The insertion of individual DOCX documents reduced the average processing time by 33.8% compared with the manual method, while insertion through ZIP files achieved a reduction of approximately 65%. The evaluated extraction parameters achieved success rates in extration ranging from 96% to 100%. Usability tests carried out with Datajuris employees also produced positive results, while simultaneously allowing the identification of aspects that may be improved in future work.

Overall, the results indicate that the proposed module provides an effective approach to reducing the effort associated with the manual processing of legal decisions, while maintaining human validation as an integral part of the workflow.
